Joanna Forrest

I am a Specialist Parkinson's Physiotherapist with a passion for rehabilitation, exercise and helping people to remain active, independent and confident while living with a neurological condition. I completed my BSc in Sports Science at the University of Wales, Swansea (now Swansea University), before graduating with a degree in Physiotherapy from the University of Birmingham in 2005.

I began my physiotherapy career within the NHS at Birmingham Heartlands and Solihull Hospitals, progressing to a senior role before moving to the Princess of Wales Community Hospital (POWCH) in Bromsgrove in 2010..

At POWCH, I worked within the Rehabilitation and Assessment Unit as a Rehabilitation and Falls Physiotherapist and also spent a year as a Specialist Community Falls Physiotherapist for Redditch and Bromsgrove. Alongside this, I developed and led the physiotherapy aspect of the Parkinson’s service, providing specialist exercise classes and one-to-one physiotherapy within the Rehabilitation and Assessment Unit.

I was involved in developing the Complex Parkinson’s Multidisciplinary Clinic, where I worked as Lead Physiotherapist, and supported the Worcestershire Parkinson’s Specialist Nurses with complex home visits as a Parkinson’s Specialist. These experiences have given me a broad understanding of the physical, functional and wider challenges that can accompany Parkinson’s, as well as the importance of individualised, holistic support.

Exercise is at the heart of my approach. I am a Level 4 Certified Postural Stability Instructor, an accredited PD Warrior Instructor and a Freestyle Fitness Yoga Instructor. I currently lead multiple weekly Parkinson’s UK Worcester Branch ‘Movers and Shakers’ exercise classes face to face and via Zoom, as well as a monthly exercise class in Redditch. I also jointly lead Physi-Yo Yoga for Parkinson’s classes in Droitwich with my professional yoga teacher colleague, Lisa Stone.

I am passionate about creating exercise environments that are welcoming, inclusive and enjoyable, while using evidence-based practice to target the specific challenges associated with Parkinson’s. For me, exercise is not simply about physical fitness — it is also about confidence, connection, independence and helping people continue to live well.

I am also the Health & Welfare Officer and Physiotherapist for Neuro Walking Football Worcestershire, providing specialist physiotherapy support to the coaching team and players. For the past four years, I have been the Physiotherapist for the England Parkinson’s Walking Football team, providing pitch-side and holistic support to players nationally and internationally. Being part of this community has reinforced my belief in the powerful role that exercise, friendship and team sport can play in building confidence, maintaining independence and helping people continue to do the things they enjoy.

I am committed to keeping my knowledge and practice up to date through evidence-based practice, continuing professional development and sharing knowledge with others. I have delivered teaching and CPD sessions to fellow healthcare professionals and regularly lecture at the University of Birmingham, sharing my specialist knowledge of exercise and Parkinson’s with physiotherapy students.

I am registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) and am a member of the Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP) and the Association of Chartered Physiotherapists in Neurology (ACPIN).

Physiotherapy aims

  • to keep active to maximise independence in everyday activities
  • to improve well-being
  • to maintain muscle strength and joint flexibility
  • to improve posture, balance and minimise risk of falls
  • to educate and empower
  • to promote self-management
  • to link with local NHS and community services
  • to support additional needs
